# Everyday CBT: course design

## Audience and scope

A proposed twelve-chapter introduction to cognitive behavioural therapy for adults
with no previous psychology knowledge. The course teaches learners to understand
a recurring pattern, choose something to try, and learn from the result.

This is introductory skills education, not professional clinical training,
diagnosis, or individual treatment. Clinical review and a learner pilot are planned
but have not taken place. Course content must accurately reflect that status.

Allow an estimated four to six hours of lessons and guided practice, plus short
field exercises, ideally spread over six to eight weeks. These are design estimates
to be revised using pilot data.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, learners should be able to:

- Separate observations from interpretations in a specific situation.
- Map thoughts, feelings, bodily sensations, behaviour, and circumstances.
- Explain how consequences can keep a pattern going.
- Examine evidence and uncertainty without forcing a positive interpretation.
- Choose a practical response that fits the problem and the person's context.
- Design a small behavioural experiment with an observable prediction.
- Review mixed results and choose a sensible next step.
- Recognise a returning pattern and use a realistic maintenance plan.

The canonical twelve-chapter outline is in `course.json` and is rendered on the site.

## Story and teaching design

Follow recurring fictional characters through everyday situations. Similar
behaviour can have different functions; real adversity should not be rewritten as
a thinking error. Include environmental constraints, boundaries, and support.
Give characters agency and avoid presenting the narrator as able to read minds.

Each chapter follows this sequence:

1. An illustrated situation that stops at a meaningful decision.
2. A learner response before the explanation.
3. A short narrated explanation, diagram, and worked example.
4. Guided practice followed by a less supported attempt.
5. Feedback on reasoning, including multiple reasonable responses where applicable.
6. An optional small field exercise and a later review prompt.
7. Recall practice that revisits earlier skills.

Early lessons use labelled diagrams and sentence starters. Later cases require
learners to select the tool and explain why it fits. Learners can complete the
whole course using fictional cases; personal disclosure is never required.

Gradual-approach activities must be voluntary and limited to safe everyday
situations. Detailed exercise design needs clinical review for scope, context,
appropriate pacing, and when tailored support is needed.

## Assessment

Three proposed independent pieces of work:

- Early: explain how a fictional character's pattern keeps going.
- Middle: design an experiment and interpret several possible outcomes.
- Final: choose an approach for an unfamiliar case, then revise it when new
  information appears.

Use self-assessment criteria for specificity, evidence, flexibility, and fit of
action to problem. Immediate emotional improvement is not the success criterion.
Multiple-choice decisions provide formative practice; they do not establish
mastery or clinical competence.

## Prototype first

Prototype Chapter 4, **Making room for another interpretation**, with an unanswered
message as its central case. Include the event/interpretation distinction, a
five-part map, a thought record, feedback on balanced reasoning, and an optional
follow-up experiment that introduces Chapter 7.

Review the prototype before producing the remaining lessons. Check whether
learners can explain their reasoning and transfer it to a different case.

## Production milestones

1. **Foundation:** repository, course overview, structured curriculum, Pages workflow.
2. **One complete prototype:** original illustrations, lesson text, worked examples,
   decisions and feedback, recall, and printable practice.
3. **Content review:** qualified CBT practitioner review and source checking;
   resolve findings before presenting the material as reviewed.
4. **Learner pilot:** observe comprehension, navigation, feedback usefulness, and
   accessibility; revise content and course time estimates.
5. **Full course:** develop and review the remaining chapters, integrated cases,
   evidence notes, and self-assessment guidance.
6. **Media and distribution:** recorded narration, visual companion, printable
   workbook, offline packages, and browser/playback verification.

Only the foundation is implemented at this stage. No lesson, recorded narration,
worksheet, assessment, clinical review, or learner pilot is complete.

## Sources and delivery

Maintain chapter-level evidence notes with verified source links, the purpose and
limits of each technique, and review dates. Do not infer evidence for this course's
effectiveness from evidence supporting CBT in other settings.

Use original material, document asset provenance and licenses, and retain source
files. Follow Common Ground's learning format without assuming rights to reuse
its course content. Build a responsive, keyboard-accessible static site with
printable materials and no requirement to submit personal writing to a server.
